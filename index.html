The big picture

India held up while the world's AI trade cracked, and October's spending numbers were the strongest in years, even with oil near $110.

In short

The trade that flipped in October

For 2 years the easy money was in AI shares. In October that reversed. AI shares fell more than 13% while the rest of the US market rose about 8%. Markets with lots of chip and AI makers, like Korea and Taiwan, fell hardest. India has few such companies, so it looked like a safe place.

What ties it together

One chain links the biggest themes. War near Hormuz keeps fuel dear. Dear fuel pushes prices up, so the RBI raised rates and may raise again. Higher rates in India and huge borrowing by US AI firms both push up the cost of money. That hurts small companies most, which is why large Indian shares won October.

02

The price of money

Rates are rising in India, lenders are passing it on, and the new gold-loan rules are showing stress that was hidden before.

In short
  • The RBI raised its key rate to 5.50% and banks think it may reach 6%.
  • Home loans are getting dearer, and gold-loan defaults jumped under new rules.
  • Abroad, the cost of money stays high because AI firms are borrowing so much.

Rates are going up, and lenders say so openly

The RBI raised the repo rate (the rate at which it lends to banks) on 7 October, its first rise since 2023. Lenders now talk about more to come.

  • Bank of Baroda — expects up to 0.50% more in this cycle, taking the repo rate to 6%.
  • LIC Housing Finance — raised lending rates by 0.25% on new and old loans; warns further hikes may hurt home-loan demand.
  • Embassy REIT — raised ₹2,050 crore at 7.14%; says interest cost holds even after 2 more hikes, but ₹4,200 crore must be refinanced in FY28.
  • SBI Research — expects the 10-year bond yield (what the government pays to borrow for 10 years) to fall to 6.75-7% by March 2027, from 7.42%.

One view is that rates are near the top: oil is cooling, bond yields fell on Monday, and SBI sees lower yields by March. The opposite view comes from the lenders themselves, who are pricing 1 or 2 more hikes into their plans.

A flood of dollar deposits sits unused

The RBI's special window for NRI dollar deposits (FCNR(B), a deposit kept in foreign currency) pulled in about $133 billion by 31 August. Bank of Baroda alone raised nearly twice its $4 billion target.

  • Stored cash — Bank of Baroda used only about ₹6,000 crore to repay costly deposits and parked the rest, buying about ₹40,000 crore of government bonds.
  • No deposit-rate rise yet — it has not raised deposit rates, because it is sitting on spare cash.
  • Loans growing — loans grew 18.5%; it raised its full-year loan growth guide to 13-15%.

Reading between the lines, this stored cash is why Indian deposit rates have not jumped even as the repo rate rose. When it is lent out, banks will need fresh deposits, and that is when deposit rates may move.

Gold loans: growth on top, stress underneath

New RBI gold-loan rules from 1 April allow monthly interest or EMI payments. Many borrowers who once paid everything at the end now miss monthly dues, so their loans show up as overdue.

03

Oil, fuel and the war

The barrel eased on peace talk, but finished fuels stay scarce, and the war may restart right after the US vote.

In short
  • Brent fell from $118 to about $111 on hopes that Iran will reopen Hormuz.
  • Diesel and jet fuel are the real shortage, because they cannot get out of the Gulf.
  • Reports say the US may resume strikes on Iran soon after the 3 November election.

Where prices stand

Brent rose sharply in October and touched $118 on Friday. By Monday morning it was near $111 on reports that Tehran may reopen the Strait of Hormuz on its own terms, while India's own crude basket sat at its highest since 2012.

The shortage is in diesel, not crude

Crude can bypass Hormuz through Saudi and UAE pipelines or ship-to-ship transfers. Finished fuels like diesel and jet fuel cannot, so Gulf refineries cut output.

  • Gulf refining — Middle East refinery runs fell from 9.9m to 7.3m barrels a day (Kpler); Saudi Arabia's Jizan refinery was shut after a Houthi attack.
  • Stocks — world oil stocks fell 509 million barrels from end-February to end-September, the lowest since 2016.
  • Fear premium — Goldman Sachs puts about $27 of the Brent price down to fear of what comes next.
  • China — cut seaborne crude imports sharply and still holds about 1.2 billion barrels in storage, which eased the squeeze.

Russia's refineries keep burning

Ukraine hit Lukoil's Kstovo refinery, Russia's 4th largest and the 3rd refinery struck in a few days. Russian refining has fallen to its lowest since 2002.

  • Price gap — Japan, Korea and Europe pay over $1,100 per thousand cubic metres of gas; China pays under $300.

  • Diplomacy — at the G20 foreign ministers' meeting, S Jaishankar said sanctions and shipping chokepoints are being used as weapons; the meeting ended with no joint statement.

The war with Iran began about 245 days ago. Reports say Washington told Gulf allies to expect a "short and intense" campaign after the 3 November vote, and that Israel's Benjamin Netanyahu is pressing for strikes right away.

India's share market

Large companies had their best month against small ones in nearly 2 years, foreign money turned buyer for a week, and the IPO pipe stayed busy.

In short
  • Big shares beat small shares by the widest gap since January 2025.
  • Foreigners still sold for the month but bought the most in 16 months in the final week of October.
  • Pharma and power led the final week of October; small IPOs cooled while Jio's giant issue did well.

Large beat small

Large companies beat mid and small companies by about 5.5 percentage points in October. Nifty closed Friday at 23,053, up about 2% for the week.

One view is that this is a classic bounce in oversold large shares; one trader who bought Nifty futures with borrowed money made 27% in under a month. The opposite view is that small companies with good results are now being rewarded too, as seen in the most recent sessions.

India's economy

October was a record month on almost every count, but import prices and festival timing explain part of the jump.

In short
  • GST, UPI, power and diesel demand all hit new highs in October.
  • GST on imports grew much faster than GST on local sales, partly because oil is dear.
  • A weak monsoon and a strong El Niño are the risks for farm incomes next year.

No room for tax relief on shares

Talk of removing capital gains tax on shares keeps coming back. The counter-argument is simple arithmetic: high oil has already added about ₹2.85 lakh crore to fuel and fertiliser subsidies, and the tax on long-term share gains brought in about ₹1.25 lakh crore in 2025. A new pay commission is also due. So investors should assume no relief.

Weather, rupee and building work

  • Monsoon — ended 12.6% below normal; IMD says a very strong El Niño peaks in November-December and lasts until March 2027.
  • Rupee vs yuan — the rupee has lost about 25% against China's yuan in 18 months; that makes Chinese goods dearer and helps Indian makers who compete with them.
  • Bullet trains — PM Narendra Modi approved a Delhi-Guwahati high-speed corridor via Agra, Lucknow, Varanasi and Patna; the first India-made bullet train runs at up to 280 kmph.
  • Sleeper trains — the Bengaluru-Mumbai Vande Bharat sleeper cuts the 1,118 km trip to about 16 hours; exports of Vande Bharat trains are planned in 3-4 years.
  • Cities — Pune Metro Line 3 opens on 4 November; Tamil Nadu launched a ₹1.5 lakh crore 5-year city works plan.
  • Air — Delhi had its cleanest October in 8 years (average AQI 166), yet India Gate read 335, "very poor", on Sunday morning.

So the economy runs on 2 engines at once: festive spending and state building work. The weak monsoon is the part that can slow rural demand once the festive season ends.

  • Many firms show big order books but weak cash flow, so check the cash.
